# Naming

**Verso** — the reverse side of a printed page. The ingredient panel is on the
back of the pack, so the name means "read the back".

## Why this one

The category is crowded with two kinds of names, and both were wrong for us.
Soft produce words — Yuka, Fig, Olive, Osana, Vireo — are wellness-coded and
blur into one another. Literal descriptors — EatSafe, TruthIn, FoodReveal — are
generic and close to untrademarkable. Verso sits in a third register:
bibliographic, calm, reference-like.

The constraint that decided it: **the name is the first claim the product
makes.** We publish claims about named companies implying they sell different
formulations to different markets. An accusatory name would undercut the entire
posture in `docs/claim-templates.md` before a user has scanned anything. Verso
points at the evidence instead of characterising the company — the same
discipline the claim templates enforce on every generated sentence.

Practical properties: five letters, two syllables, pronounceable in Hindi,
Spanish, Portuguese, Indonesian and English. Reads as credible rather than
militant, which also suits the institutional licensing line for the Evidence
Layer.

Cost, stated honestly: the meaning is opaque on first contact. Verso needs a
tagline doing real work wherever the name appears cold.

## Open actions before this is committed to

- [ ] Trademark search in **class 9** (software) and **class 42** (SaaS) in IN,
      US, EU and GB. Verso Books holds marks in publishing classes — different
      classes, likely survivable, but this needs a professional opinion rather
      than a search-engine result.
- [ ] Domain: `verso.app` / `getverso.*` / `verso.food`
- [ ] Handles on the platforms where the comparison screenshots will spread
- [ ] Reserve the iOS/Android bundle ID (proposed: `app.verso.scanner`)
- [ ] Check the word carries no unfortunate meaning in target-market languages

## Naming rules for anything downstream

Feature names follow the same restraint as the product name. Describe what the
user sees, never what a company intended. The cross-market feature is
**Compare**, not "Exposé" or "Double Standard" — the screen shows two labels and
lets the reader draw the conclusion.
